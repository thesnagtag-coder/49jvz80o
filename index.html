Before you start - safety first

HSE
If there is a fire: get out, stay out, and call your local emergency number from outside. Never go back inside. (Professional practice)
  • Pressing an alarm's test button is loud. In Pre-Purchase mode, ask first. (Professional practice)
  • Do not stand on chairs or furniture to reach alarms. Use a stable step or ask for help. (Professional practice)
  • Test alarms with the test button. (NFPA Smoke Alarms) Never use a flame or smoke to test. (Professional practice)
  • Electrical warning signs (warm outlets, burning smells, sparks) belong in the Electrical check and need a qualified electrician. (Professional practice)

Room Sweep

SHEET 01 ·

Walk the home from each bedroom to the front door, as if escaping. Look up for alarms, and around for anything that could start a fire or block the way out. For each area, also ask: is anything hidden, covered or blocked?

    What this tool will not do

    LIMITS
    • It is not a formal fire risk assessment, and it does not confirm compliance with any law.
    • It does not test electrical, gas or heating installations. Use the Electrical check, and qualified professionals.
    • Landlord, apartment building and holiday-let fire rules differ by country and are not covered here.

    Standards & Measurements

    REFERENCES

    Guidance used in this tool

    • Where alarms go: in every bedroom, outside each sleeping area, on every level, and in the basement; on the ceiling or high on a wall; at least 3 m (10 ft) from the stove. (NFPA Smoke Alarms, USFA Smoke Alarms)
    • Testing and replacement: test all smoke alarms at least once a month; replace alarms when they are 10 years old; replace 9-volt batteries at least once a year. (NFPA Smoke Alarms, USFA Smoke Alarms)
    • Interconnected alarms: when one sounds, they all sound. (NFPA Smoke Alarms, USFA Smoke Alarms)
    • Never disable an alarm for cooking: open a window or door and press the hush button instead. (USFA Smoke Alarms)
    • Escape: find 2 ways out of every room; choose an outside meeting place; practise with everyone. People could have less than 2 minutes to escape once the alarm sounds. High-rise: use stairs, never lifts. (USFA Escape Plans)
    • Close the door: in UL FSRI tests, closed-door bedrooms stayed below about 38 °C (100 °F) while open-door rooms exceeded about 538 °C (1,000 °F). (UL FSRI Close Before You Doze)
    • Heating: keep anything that can burn at least 0.9 m (3 ft) from heat sources; turn space heaters off when leaving the room or going to bed; plug heaters directly into the wall; have heating and chimneys cleaned and inspected each year by a professional; never heat the home with an oven. (USFA Heating)
    • Cooking: unattended equipment was the leading factor in US non-confined home cooking fires (37%). Stand by your pan; if you leave the kitchen, turn the burner off. (USFA Cooking)
    • Dryers: clean the lint filter before and after each cycle; clean behind the dryer; check the vent is not damaged, crushed or restricted; make sure the outdoor vent flap opens. (USFA Dryers)
    • UK: fit at least one smoke alarm on every level; test at least monthly; heat alarms suit kitchens. You are around 11 times more likely to die in a fire with no working smoke alarms. (UK Fire Kills)
    • Units: 1 m = 3.28 ft. Metric first, imperial in brackets.
    Country-specific: legal requirements for alarms (types, locations, landlord duties), emergency numbers and building fire rules differ by country and region. This tool uses public safety guidance, not law. Check with your local fire service.

    Items marked Professional practice reflect general HSE practice. No single public standard covers them, so no standard is claimed.

      Before you start: how your information is saved

      Your information is saved on this device only.

      Save a backup file regularly.

      Where is my information saved?

      Inside this browser, on this device. Nothing is sent to The Snag Tag or anyone else. That keeps it private, but it also means you are the only one who can keep a copy safe.

      Why could my information seem to disappear?
      • You open the tool in a different browser, a private/incognito window, or on another device.
      • You open a different copy of the tool (for example a downloaded file instead of your link).
      • Your browsing history or site data gets cleared.
      • Safari (iPhone, iPad and Mac) can delete saved information if you don't open the tool for about 7 days.
      • The tool is open in two tabs at the same time. Always use one tab.
      How do I keep my information safe?
      • Press Save a backup now after every session where you change something.
      • Keep the backup file somewhere safe, like your cloud drive or a USB stick.
      • Always open the tool from the same link, in the same browser.
      How do I get my information back, or use another device?

      Open the tool, press Restore a backup and choose your latest backup file. Everything comes back as it was when you saved it.
